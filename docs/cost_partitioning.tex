\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Cost partitioning admits the sum of several admissible heuristics as an
admissible heuristic, provided that the shares assigned to each action sum to
at most the cost of that action. Saturated cost partitioning constructs those
shares greedily: each component in turn retains the least cost function that
preserves its own estimates, and the remainder is passed to the next component.
The construction is admissible for every ordering of the components, but its
accuracy is not, and this article reports a controlled measurement of that
dependence within a verification framework in which exact goal distances are
available by exhaustive enumeration. Three constructions over the same set of
goal-pattern databases are compared against two admissible controls on
Blocksworld instances of seven blocks, over the $263\,958$ reachable states for
which $\hstar$ is computed exactly. A uniform partition is admissible and is
the least accurate heuristic measured, at mean informedness $0.195$ against
$0.339$ for the maximum over the same components. A single saturated ordering
attains the same mean informedness as that maximum while expanding
$50.8\%$ more nodes under A\textsuperscript{*}, and a witness state is
exhibited at which its estimate is strictly below the maximum. The maximum over
the $k$ cyclic rotations of the ordering is shown to dominate the maximum over
the components at every state, and attains informedness $0.420$ with $28.7\%$
fewer expansions. A heterogeneous chain, in which the databases saturate and a
landmark bound is priced with the residual cost function, dominates both
controls by construction, and improves on the stronger of them by $1.9\%$ of
informedness. An accompanying analysis attributes the size of that margin to
the distribution of cost mass: the databases retain between $70.4\%$ and
$78.6\%$ of the total action cost and yield the better bound at $20.9\%$ of the
evaluated states. Finally, every ordering is enumerated at five and six blocks,
where $k!$ is $120$ and $720$. The best single ordering, selected with full
knowledge of the state space, remains approximately $20\%$ below the rotation
family, and the rotation family recovers $98.6\%$ and $99.2\%$ of the
informedness of the maximum over all $k!$ orderings. The accuracy of the
construction therefore resides in taking a maximum over a family covering every
component, and not in the choice of ordering.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Optimal classical planning by A\textsuperscript{*} search~\cite{hart1968}
requires an admissible heuristic, and the accuracy of that heuristic determines
the number of node expansions required. Several families of admissible
heuristics are available, including delete relaxations~\cite{bonet2001},
critical-path heuristics~\cite{haslum2000}, landmark
heuristics~\cite{karpas2009}, and abstraction heuristics, of which pattern
databases~\cite{culberson1998,edelkamp2001,haslum2007} are the oldest and the
most widely implemented. Given several admissible heuristics, their pointwise
maximum is admissible and is the standard way of combining them. The maximum,
however, discards the information carried by every component that does not
attain it at the state being evaluated.

Cost partitioning~\cite{katz2008,yang2008,pommerening2015} provides a
combination that can strictly exceed the maximum. Each component is evaluated
under its own cost function over actions, and the sum of the components is
admissible whenever the shares assigned to an action sum to at most the cost of
that action in the task. The bound holds because no two components can charge
for the same action twice. Saturated cost
partitioning~\cite{seipp2014,seipp2020} is a specific and inexpensive scheme
for producing such shares. Each component in turn is assigned the least cost
function under which it retains all of its own estimates, which for an
abstraction heuristic is computable from the abstract transition relation, and
the residual cost function is passed to the next component. The scheme is
greedy and therefore depends on the order in which the components are
considered.

This article reports a measurement of that dependence in a setting in which the
ground truth is available. The verification harness of the framework enumerates
the reachable state space of an instance and computes $\hstar$ at every state by
backward Dijkstra search from the goal states, so that admissibility is decided
and accuracy is measured directly instead of being inferred from search
behaviour. Within that setting the following are reported.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt,leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item A formal statement of the saturated cost function for a projection
        abstraction, with the treatment of transitions incident to abstract
        dead ends made explicit (Section~\ref{sec:saturation}), and a proof
        that a chain of such components spends the cost function of the task
        exactly once (Theorem~\ref{thm:chain}).
  \item A witness demonstrating that a single saturated ordering does not
        dominate the maximum over its own components
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:nodomination}), together with the measured
        consequence: identical mean informedness and $50.8\%$ more A\textsuperscript{*}
        expansions than the maximum.
  \item A proof that the maximum over the $k$ cyclic rotations of an ordering
        dominates the maximum over the components at every state
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations}), and its measured effect.
  \item A heterogeneous chain combining pattern databases with a landmark
        bound, shown to dominate both controls by construction
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed}), and an analysis attributing the size of its
        empirical margin to the distribution of cost mass among the components
        (Section~\ref{sec:anatomy}).
  \item An exhaustive enumeration of the $k!$ orderings at the instance sizes
        where it is feasible, bounding both what a single selected ordering and
        what a larger family of orderings can contribute
        (Section~\ref{sec:orders}).
\end{enumerate}

The constructions in items~1, 3 and~4 are not new; Section~\ref{sec:related}
attributes each to the literature. The contribution of this article is the
independent implementation, the exact verification of every construction
against enumerated goal distances, and the quantitative attribution of the
observed margins.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Planning tasks}

A propositional STRIPS planning task is a tuple
$\Task = \langle P, A, s_0, G \rangle$, where $P$ is a finite set of
propositions, $A$ a finite set of actions, $s_0 \subseteq P$ the initial state
and $G \subseteq P$ the goal condition. A state is a subset $s \subseteq P$. An
action $a = \langle \pre(a), \add(a), \del(a) \rangle$ with
$\pre(a), \add(a), \del(a) \subseteq P$ is applicable in $s$ if
$\pre(a) \subseteq s$, and yields the successor
\[
  s\llbracket a \rrbracket \;=\; \bigl( s \setminus \del(a) \bigr) \cup \add(a).
\]
A cost function is a map $c : A \to \R_{\ge 0}$. A state $s$ is a goal state if
$G \subseteq s$. A plan from $s$ is a sequence of actions leading from $s$ to a
goal state, and $\hstar_c(s)$ denotes the minimum cost of such a sequence under
$c$, with $\hstar_c(s) = \infty$ when no plan exists. A heuristic
$h : 2^{P} \to \R \cup \{\infty\}$ is \emph{admissible} for $c$ if
$h(s) \le \hstar_c(s)$ for every state $s$, and \emph{consistent} if
$h(s) \le c(a) + h(s\llbracket a \rrbracket)$ for every $s$ and every $a$
applicable in $s$. Consistency together with $h(s) = 0$ at goal states implies
admissibility. Where the cost function is that of the task it is omitted, and
$\hstar$ abbreviates $\hstar_c$.

\subsection{Cost partitioning}

\begin{definition}[Cost partition]
\label{def:partition}
A tuple $\langle c_1, \dots, c_k \rangle$ of cost functions is a
\emph{cost partition} of $c$ if $c_i(a) \ge 0$ for all $i$ and $a$, and
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{k} c_i(a) \;\le\; c(a) \qquad \text{for every } a \in A .
\]
\end{definition}

Cost may be left unspent; only overspending invalidates the bound. The
following is the standard justification of the scheme and is stated here
because every construction in Sections~\ref{sec:saturation}
to~\ref{sec:mixed} is an instance of it.

\begin{proposition}[Additivity under a cost partition]
\label{prop:additivity}
Let $\langle c_1, \dots, c_k \rangle$ be a cost partition of $c$ and let $h_i$
be admissible for $c_i$ for each $i$. Then $\sum_{i=1}^{k} h_i$ is admissible
for $c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be a state with $\hstar_c(s) < \infty$ and let
$\langle a_1, \dots, a_m \rangle$ be a cost-optimal plan from $s$ under $c$.
The sequence is a plan under every cost function, so admissibility of $h_i$
under $c_i$ gives $h_i(s) \le \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_i(a_j)$. Summing over $i$ and
exchanging the order of summation,
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{k} h_i(s) \;\le\; \sum_{j=1}^{m} \sum_{i=1}^{k} c_i(a_j)
  \;\le\; \sum_{j=1}^{m} c(a_j) \;=\; \hstar_c(s).
\]
The inequality is vacuous where $\hstar_c(s) = \infty$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:additivity} places no requirement on the components
beyond admissibility under their own shares. In particular the components need
not be of the same family, a fact exploited in Section~\ref{sec:mixed}, and the
shares may be chosen as a function of the state, which is what makes optimal
cost partitioning by linear programming~\cite{katz2008,pommerening2014} well
defined.

The following observation delimits the schemes that can be useful and motivates
the general form.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform scaling cannot exceed the maximum]
\label{prop:scaling}
Let $h_1, \dots, h_k$ be admissible for $c$ and let $w_1, \dots, w_k \ge 0$
with $\sum_i w_i \le 1$. Let $h_i^{w_i}$ denote $h_i$ evaluated under the cost
function $w_i \cdot c$. Then
$\sum_i h_i^{w_i}(s) \le \max_j h_j(s)$ for every $s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Scaling every action cost by $w_i$ scales the goal distance, and hence the
value of an abstraction or landmark bound built under it, by the same factor,
so $h_i^{w_i} = w_i h_i$. Therefore
$\sum_i w_i h_i(s) \le (\sum_i w_i) \max_j h_j(s) \le \max_j h_j(s)$.
\end{proof}

Since $\max_j h_j$ is itself admissible, a partition of the shape covered by
Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling} is never preferable to the maximum. Only
partitions in which components receive genuinely different cost functions over
actions can exceed it.

\subsection{Projection abstractions}

Let $\Phi \subseteq P$ be a \emph{pattern}. The projection onto $\Phi$ maps a
state to $s \cap \Phi$ and an action $a$ to
\[
  \proj(a) \;=\; \langle\, \pre(a) \cap \Phi,\; \add(a) \cap \Phi,\;
                          \del(a) \cap \Phi \,\rangle .
\]
The projection is a homomorphism of the transition system, since intersection
distributes over the STRIPS transition and a projected precondition is weaker
than the concrete one. Every concrete plan therefore induces an abstract plan
of the same cost, the abstract goal distance is a lower bound on the concrete
one, and the resulting heuristic is admissible and consistent. A \emph{pattern
database} stores the abstract goal distance of every abstract state and
evaluates by masking and lookup~\cite{culberson1998,edelkamp2001}. Actions
with $\add(a) \cap \Phi = \del(a) \cap \Phi = \emptyset$ induce self-loops in
the abstraction and are discarded; write $A_\Phi$ for the set of actions
retained.

Patterns here are constructed by bounded causal closure: each goal condition
seeds one pattern, the preconditions of the actions achieving the seed are
added, then the preconditions of the actions achieving those, up to a budget of
twelve propositions. Pattern selection is a research problem in its own
right~\cite{haslum2007,seipp2018} and no claim is made for this procedure
beyond determinism and adequacy as a source of components.

\section{Saturated cost partitioning}
\label{sec:saturation}

\subsection{The saturated cost function}

A pattern database stores goal distances computed under one cost function. If
the stored table is to be read while a smaller cost function is charged for,
the table must remain a bound under that smaller function.

\begin{definition}[Saturated cost function]
\label{def:scf}
Let $D_i$ be a pattern database over pattern $\Phi_i$, built under a cost
function $d$, with abstract goal distances $h_i$. Its \emph{saturated cost
function} $\scf_i : A \to \R_{\ge 0}$ is
\begin{align*}
  \scf_i(a)   &= \min\bigl(\, d(a),\; \delta_i(a) \,\bigr), \\
  \delta_i(a) &= \max\bigl( \{0\} \cup \Delta_i(a) \bigr), \\
  \Delta_i(a) &= \bigl\{\, h_i(u) - h_i(v) \;\bigm|\; \\
              &\qquad u \xrightarrow{\;\proj(a)\;} v,\;
                       h_i(u), h_i(v) < \infty \,\bigr\},
\end{align*}
where the transitions range over the abstract transition relation and
$\scf_i(a) = 0$ for every $a \notin A_\Phi$.
\end{definition}

Two families of transition are excluded from $\Delta_i(a)$ and the exclusions
are not cosmetic. A transition with $h_i(u) = \infty$ leaves an abstract dead
end, which no search expands, and the difference is undefined. A transition
with $h_i(v) = \infty$ enters an abstract dead end, so that $h_i(u) - h_i(v)$
is unbounded below and the constraint it imposes is satisfied by every
non-negative value. Charging for either transition would remove cost from
subsequent components without improving the present one.

\begin{theorem}[Saturation preserves the table]
\label{thm:saturation}
$h_i$ is admissible for the projected task under $\scf_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every abstract goal state satisfies $h_i = 0$. For every abstract transition
$u \xrightarrow{\proj(a)} v$ with $h_i(u), h_i(v) < \infty$,
Definition~\ref{def:scf} gives $\scf_i(a) \ge h_i(u) - h_i(v)$, hence
$h_i(u) \le \scf_i(a) + h_i(v)$; where $h_i(v) = \infty$ the inequality is
immediate. Thus $h_i$ is consistent and goal aware under $\scf_i$.

Let $u_0$ be an abstract state with $\hstar_{\scf_i}(u_0) < \infty$ and let
$\langle a_1, \dots, a_m \rangle$ be a cost-optimal plan under $\scf_i$ through
$u_0, \dots, u_m$ with $u_m$ an abstract goal state. Every $u_j$ lies on a path
to a goal state, so $h_i(u_j) < \infty$ for all $j$. Telescoping the
consistency inequality along the path,
\[
  h_i(u_0) \;\le\; \sum_{j=1}^{m} \scf_i(a_j) + h_i(u_m)
           \;=\; \hstar_{\scf_i}(u_0). \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The clipping at $d(a)$ in Definition~\ref{def:scf} is what makes $\scf_i$ a
share of $d$ and not a demand upon it. A drop exceeding $d(a)$ is possible,
because the projection may identify several concrete actions with one abstract
action; retaining the unclipped drop would permit a component to charge more
for an action than the task ever pays for it.

\subsection{Chains}

\begin{definition}[Saturated chain]
\label{def:chain}
Given an ordered sequence of patterns $\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_k$, set $r_0 = c$
and, for $i = 1, \dots, k$: build $D_i$ under $r_{i-1}$, let $c_i = \scf_i$
computed under $r_{i-1}$, and set $r_i = r_{i-1} - c_i$. The heuristic is
$h_{\mathrm{scp}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} h_i$, and $r_k$ is the \emph{residual} cost
function of the chain.
\end{definition}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Saturated chain over an ordered pattern sequence}
\label{alg:chain}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require task $\Task$ with cost function $c$; patterns $\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_k$
\Ensure databases $D_1, \dots, D_k$; shares $c_1, \dots, c_k$; residual $r$
\State $r \gets c$
\For{$i \gets 1$ \textbf{to} $k$}
  \State $D_i \gets \textsc{BuildDatabase}(\Task, \Phi_i, r)$
  \State $c_i \gets \textsc{Saturate}(D_i, r)$
    \Comment{Definition~\ref{def:scf}}
  \State $r \gets r - c_i$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[The chain spends the cost function exactly once]
\label{thm:chain}
For every action $a$: $c_i(a) \ge 0$ for all $i$, $r_k(a) \ge 0$, and
$\sum_{i=1}^{k} c_i(a) + r_k(a) = c(a)$. In particular
$\langle c_1, \dots, c_k \rangle$ and $\langle c_1, \dots, c_k, r_k \rangle$
are cost partitions of $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $i$. Definition~\ref{def:scf} clips $c_i$ at the cost function
under which $D_i$ was built, so $0 \le c_i(a) \le r_{i-1}(a)$, and therefore
$r_i(a) = r_{i-1}(a) - c_i(a)$ is non-negative and the subtraction is exact.
Telescoping, $\sum_{i \le k} c_i(a) = r_0(a) - r_k(a) = c(a) - r_k(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:chain-admissible}
$h_{\mathrm{scp}}$ is admissible and consistent for $\Task$ under $c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Admissibility follows from Theorems~\ref{thm:saturation} and~\ref{thm:chain}
with Proposition~\ref{prop:additivity}. Each $h_i$ is an abstraction heuristic
and is consistent under $c_i$; a sum of functions consistent under the members
of a cost partition is consistent under $c$, since summing the individual
consistency inequalities yields
$\sum_i h_i(s) \le \sum_i c_i(a) + \sum_i h_i(s\llbracket a \rrbracket)
\le c(a) + \sum_i h_i(s\llbracket a \rrbracket)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Order dependence}
\label{sec:order}

Corollary~\ref{cor:chain-admissible} holds for every ordering of the patterns.
Accuracy does not, and the failure is not confined to contrived tasks.

\begin{theorem}[A saturated chain does not dominate the maximum]
\label{thm:nodomination}
There exist a task $\Task$, a set of patterns and a state $s$ such that
$h_{\mathrm{scp}}(s) < \max_i h_i^{\,c}(s)$, where $h_i^{\,c}$ denotes the
database over $\Phi_i$ built under the cost function of the task.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P = \{\code{start}, \code{left}, \code{right}\}$,
$s_0 = \{\code{start}\}$, $G = \{\code{left}, \code{right}\}$, and let $A$
consist of three unit-cost actions: \code{both}, with precondition
$\{\code{start}\}$ and add effect $\{\code{left}, \code{right}\}$;
\code{take\_left} and \code{take\_right}, each with precondition
$\{\code{start}\}$ and add effect the corresponding goal proposition. Bounded
causal closure from each goal condition yields
$\Phi_1 = \{\code{start}, \code{left}\}$ and
$\Phi_2 = \{\code{start}, \code{right}\}$.

Consider $s = \{\code{start}, \code{left}\}$, at which $\hstar(s) = 1$. Under
$c$, the database over $\Phi_1$ reports $h_1^{\,c}(s) = 0$, since its abstract
goal $\{\code{left}\}$ is already satisfied, and the database over $\Phi_2$
reports $h_2^{\,c}(s) = 1$. The maximum over the components is therefore $1$.

Take the ordering $\langle \Phi_1, \Phi_2 \rangle$. The abstraction of $\Phi_1$
contains the transition
$\{\code{start}\} \xrightarrow{\code{both}} \{\code{start}, \code{left}\}$,
along which $h_1$ decreases from $1$ to $0$, so
$\scf_1(\code{both}) = 1$ and $r_1(\code{both}) = 0$. The database over
$\Phi_2$ is then built with \code{both} free of charge and reaches its abstract
goal from $\{\code{start}\}$ at cost $0$, giving $h_2(s) = 0$. Hence
$h_{\mathrm{scp}}(s) = h_1(s) + h_2(s) = 0 < 1$.
\end{proof}

Both values are admissible; only one is informative. The mechanism is general:
a component that has already reached its own abstract goal at $s$ nonetheless
saturates against the transitions leading into that goal, and the cost it
retains is precisely the cost the next component required.

\begin{definition}[Rotation family]
\label{def:rotations}
For patterns $\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_k$ let $\sigma_i$ denote the cyclic rotation
beginning at $\Phi_i$, and let $h^{(i)}$ be the saturated chain under
$\sigma_i$. The \emph{rotation family} heuristic is
$h_{\mathrm{rot}} = \max_{1 \le i \le k} h^{(i)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Rotations dominate the maximum]
\label{thm:rotations}
$h_{\mathrm{rot}}$ is admissible, and
$h_{\mathrm{rot}}(s) \ge \max_{1 \le i \le k} h_i^{\,c}(s)$ at every state $s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Admissibility follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:chain-admissible} applied to each
rotation and from the fact that a pointwise maximum of admissible heuristics is
admissible. For the domination, fix $i$. In $\sigma_i$ the component $\Phi_i$
is first, hence is built under $r_0 = c$, and its contribution to $h^{(i)}$ is
exactly $h_i^{\,c}(s)$. Every remaining summand of $h^{(i)}$ is an abstract
goal distance and is therefore non-negative, so
$h^{(i)}(s) \ge h_i^{\,c}(s)$. Taking the maximum over $i$ on both sides gives
the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:minimality}
The rotation family is minimal among order families with this property in the
following sense: removing $\sigma_i$ removes the only ordering in which
$\Phi_i$ is unpriced by any other component, and the argument above no longer
covers a state at which $\Phi_i$ attains the maximum. It is not the only such
family; any set of permutations in which every component appears first at least
once suffices, and the rotations are the smallest such set. Selecting a smaller
and better set is a search over permutations and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
\end{remark}

\section{Heterogeneous partitions}
\label{sec:mixed}

Proposition~\ref{prop:additivity} does not require the components to belong to
one family. The two admissible components available in this framework have
complementary profiles: the databases are consistent, evaluate in constant
time, and capture interactions internal to a pattern; the landmark bound is
better informed, is inconsistent, and requires one relaxed reachability
fixpoint per candidate proposition at each evaluation. The landmark bound used
here is the uniform cost partition over the landmarks of a
state~\cite{karpas2009}: each action divides its cost equally among the
landmarks it may achieve, and each landmark contributes the least share
assigned to it by any of its achievers.

A landmark bound of this kind admits no saturated cost function in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:scf}. An abstraction stores a function over the whole
abstract state space, so the least cost function preserving it is a property of
the stored table; the landmarks of a state are recomputed at that state, and
there is no stored function to preserve. The analogue would be a distinct cost
function at each state, for which the databases cannot be rebuilt. The chain
therefore admits only one arrangement: the databases saturate, and the landmark
bound is priced with the residual $r_k$, which requires no saturation because
no component follows it.

\begin{definition}[Mixed family]
\label{def:mixed}
For each rotation $\sigma_i$ let $h^{(i)}$ be its saturated chain over the
databases and $r^{(i)}$ its residual. Writing $h_{\mathrm{LM}}^{\,d}$ for the
landmark bound priced by cost function $d$,
\[
  h_{\mathrm{mix}}(s) = \max\Bigl( h_{\mathrm{LM}}^{\,c}(s),\;
  \max_{1 \le i \le k} \bigl[ h^{(i)}(s) + h_{\mathrm{LM}}^{\,r^{(i)}}(s) \bigr]
  \Bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Domination of both controls]
\label{thm:mixed}
$h_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is admissible, and for every state $s$,
$h_{\mathrm{mix}}(s) \ge \max_i h_i^{\,c}(s)$ and
$h_{\mathrm{mix}}(s) \ge h_{\mathrm{LM}}^{\,c}(s)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:chain} the tuple
$\langle c_1^{(i)}, \dots, c_k^{(i)}, r^{(i)} \rangle$ is a cost partition of
$c$, so each bracketed sum is admissible by
Proposition~\ref{prop:additivity}. The first argument of the outer maximum is
the degenerate partition assigning the entire cost function to the landmark
bound, which is admissible. A pointwise maximum of admissible heuristics is
admissible.

The landmark control is dominated because it is a member of the family. The
abstraction control is dominated by the argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations}, since the $i$-th bracket contains
$h^{(i)}(s) \ge h_i^{\,c}(s)$ together with a non-negative landmark term.
\end{proof}

The degenerate member is required only for the second half of the proof, and
costs one additional pricing of a landmark set that has already been generated.
Since the landmark set of a state is determined by relaxed reachability and is
independent of the cost function, generation is performed once per state and
the set is priced $k+1$ times.

\begin{remark}
$h_{\mathrm{mix}}$ inherits the inconsistency of the landmark bound.
A\textsuperscript{*} with an inconsistent heuristic may reopen closed states,
and Table~\ref{tab:comparison} reports the frequency; optimality is preserved
by reopening.
\end{remark}

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

All constructions are header-only additions to an existing C\texttt{++}20
planning framework in which cost functions were already first-class: landmark
generation, database construction and exact goal-distance computation each
accept a supplied cost function, and a predicate deciding
Definition~\ref{def:partition} was already available. Three changes were
required before Definition~\ref{def:scf} could be implemented.

\paragraph{Recovering concrete action indices.}
A projection discards the actions of $A \setminus A_\Phi$, so the action
indices of the abstract task are not those of the concrete task. A saturated
cost function is computed over abstract transitions and must be expressed over
$A$, so the projection now records the concrete origin of each retained action.

\paragraph{Naming the retention predicate.}
The test $\add(a) \cap \Phi \neq \emptyset \lor \del(a) \cap \Phi \neq
\emptyset$ was previously evaluated inside the projection loop. The uniform
scheme requires the answer before any projection exists, in order to count the
components able to use each action, so the predicate is now a named function
that the projection calls.

\paragraph{Separating landmark generation from pricing.}
Landmark generation is independent of the cost function and is the expensive
half of the landmark bound. Pricing is now a separate entry point taking a cost
function, so one factory serves the $k+1$ shares required by
Definition~\ref{def:mixed} at the cost of one generation per state.

The saturated cost function is computed in a single pass over the abstract
transition relation at construction time, so building a database is
asymptotically no more expensive than before. The unit test suite was extended
from $87$ to $104$ tests. The additions verify that each saturated share is
bounded by the cost function it was derived from, that share and residual sum
to that function exactly (Theorem~\ref{thm:chain}), that each database remains
admissible when verified against goal distances recomputed under its own
saturated costs (Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation}), that every construction yields
a cost partition (Definition~\ref{def:partition}), that the witness of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nodomination} holds as an exact pair of values, and that the
domination of Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations} holds at every state of three
fixtures.

\section{Empirical evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Experimental setup}

The benchmark comprises the four Blocksworld~\cite{slaney2001} instances of
seven blocks used as the verification set of the framework. Each has $98$
actions and $7$ goal conditions, hence yields $7$ patterns under a budget of
twelve propositions, so the rotation and mixed families build $49$ databases
per instance. The reachable state spaces total $263\,958$ states, of which
$263\,954$ have a finite non-zero goal distance.

Two measurements are reported. \emph{Informedness} is the mean of $h/\hstar$
over states with finite non-zero $\hstar$, computed by enumerating the
reachable state space and running backward Dijkstra search from the goal
states; it requires no search and is deterministic. \emph{Expansions} are the
totals over the four instances of A\textsuperscript{*} run to optimality, with
tie-breaking in the open list by insertion order. Every configuration returns
optimal plans of total cost $60$.

Wall-clock time is separated into \emph{setup}, the interval preceding the first
node expansion, which for the abstraction heuristics is the enumeration of
every projection, and \emph{search}. The separation is necessary because the
rotation and mixed families build $k$ chains of $k$ databases, and it is that
component of the cost which grows. All measurements were taken with
g\texttt{++}~11.4.0 at C\texttt{++}20 in a release build on a single core.

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} reports the comparison. Four observations follow.

\paragraph{A legal partition is not by itself valuable.}
The uniform partition satisfies Definition~\ref{def:partition} in its general
form and is the least accurate heuristic measured, at informedness $0.195$
against $0.339$ for the maximum over the same components, with $3.75$ times as
many expansions. Dividing the cost of an action among the components able to
use it, without reference to what any of them can spend, underpays all of them
simultaneously.

\paragraph{A mean conceals a bound that collapses.}
The single saturated ordering and the maximum report identical mean
informedness to three decimal places, yet the former expands $50.8\%$ more
nodes. The minimum column identifies the cause: at $0.000$, the mechanism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nodomination} is present at scale. A mean over states conceals
a bound that falls to zero at some of them, and A\textsuperscript{*} does not,
because those states are precisely the ones it must then expand.

\paragraph{The rotation family improves accuracy and increases wall-clock time
at both ends.}
Informedness improves by $23.9\%$ over the maximum and expansions fall by
$28.7\%$, with the minimum rising from $0.111$ to $0.167$, which is the floor
guaranteed by Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations}. Setup rises from $0.24$\,s to
$1.71$\,s, a factor of approximately $k$, and search rises from $0.03$\,s to
$0.09$\,s despite the reduction in expansions, because a single evaluation
requires $k^2 = 49$ table lookups instead of $k = 7$. At seven blocks the
entire search occupies a tenth of a second, so no amortisation occurs; the
construction is a loss in wall-clock time and a gain in the quantity the
framework optimises.

\paragraph{The mixed family dominates both controls, with a small margin.}
Informedness is $0.530$ against $0.520$ and expansions $4\,067$ against
$4\,166$. Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed} guarantees the sign of both differences;
their magnitude is $1.9\%$ and $2.4\%$ respectively. Search time doubles, since
each evaluation prices the landmark set $k+1$ times in addition to performing
$k^2$ table lookups.

for a chain, because the cost required to preserve a component's table is not
the cost required for that component to be informative: a component that has
reached its abstract goal preserves a table of zeros at full price. The seven
goal-condition patterns of a Blocksworld instance overlap substantially, and
each preserves a quantity of cost that the landmark bound would have priced
more productively.

\subsection{Exhaustive enumeration of orderings}

components. A separate question is how much of the accuracy available to an

every permutation can be scored at five and six blocks: $5! = 120$ and

\paragraph{No single ordering can replace the family.}

rotation family. Selecting one ordering well is therefore not an alternative to
evaluating the family, and a procedure that searches the space of permutations
for a single chain optimises toward a target below what $k$ chains already
provide. The spread between the worst and the best ordering is nonetheless
large, at $0.3015$ to $0.5299$ over the five-block instances, so the ordering
governs the accuracy of an individual chain while contributing almost nothing
once a covering maximum is taken.

one per cent.}
The rotation family recovers $98.6\%$ of the informedness of the maximum over
all $k!$ orderings at five blocks and $99.2\%$ at six, and the latter exceeds
the former strictly at $8.5\%$ and $5.4\%$ of states respectively. The
guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations} is therefore obtained at close to the
best value any family of orderings of these components can attain, and the
selection of a larger or better-chosen family is not a productive direction for
this set of components.

Taken together the two observations locate the value of the construction in the
maximum over a family that covers every component, and not in the ordering. The
remaining accuracy, if any, lies in the components or in a partitioning rule
other than saturation.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{No upper reference point is reported.}
Optimal cost partitioning, computed per state by a linear program over the
shares~\cite{katz2008,pommerening2014}, is the standard reference against which
the recovery of a partitioning scheme is measured. It is not implemented here,
so the values $0.420$ and $0.530$ are comparisons against the controls and not
against the bound attainable from these components. Establishing that reference
is the first item of further work.

\paragraph{A single domain and small instances.}
All measurements are on Blocksworld, and seven blocks is the largest size at
which exhaustive enumeration remains inexpensive. Both constructions are
exponential in the pattern size and the rotation family is quadratic in the
number of goal conditions, so the reported setup times are lower bounds on what
a larger domain would require. In particular, the claim that the databases
retain roughly three quarters of the cost mass is a claim about this domain and
these patterns, and a domain whose goal conditions interact less would be
expected to behave differently.

\paragraph{Pattern selection is not addressed.}
One pattern per goal condition is generated by bounded causal closure to twelve
propositions. Raising that budget continues to improve informedness at seven
blocks, which indicates that pattern selection influences the result at least
as strongly as the partition over the patterns. A scheme that allocates cost
well among poorly selected components remains limited by those components.

\paragraph{The cost of the rotation family is not avoidable by selection.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations} obtains its guarantee by evaluating all $k$
orderings, and a single ordering would cost a $k$-th of the setup.
Section~\ref{sec:orders} shows that no such saving is available: the best
ordering over all $k!$, selected with full knowledge of the state space, is
approximately $20\%$ below the family. The residual cost of the construction is
therefore intrinsic to it at these sizes, and reducing it requires cheaper
components and not a cheaper family.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Pattern databases were introduced for the sliding-tile puzzle by Culberson and
Schaeffer~\cite{culberson1998} and brought to domain-independent planning by
Edelkamp~\cite{edelkamp2001}, with automatic pattern selection developed by
Haslum et al.~\cite{haslum2007}. Additivity of abstraction heuristics under
disjoint action costs was formalised by Yang et al.~\cite{yang2008}, and the
general form of cost partitioning, together with its optimal computation by
linear programming, is due to Katz and Domshlak~\cite{katz2008}; the extension
to general, possibly negative, operator cost partitioning is due to Pommerening
et al.~\cite{pommerening2015}. The uniform cost partition over landmarks used
as the second component here is that of Karpas and
Domshlak~\cite{karpas2009}, and the relationship between landmark and
abstraction heuristics under cost partitioning was examined by Helmert and
Domshlak~\cite{helmert2009}.

Saturated cost partitioning is due to Seipp and Helmert~\cite{seipp2014}, who
introduced it for Cartesian abstractions, and is developed at length by Seipp,
Keller and Helmert~\cite{seipp2020}. Its dependence on the ordering of the
components, and the use of several orderings selected for diversity, are
treated in the same line of work~\cite{seipp2017,seipp2020}; the rotation
family of Definition~\ref{def:rotations} is a specific deterministic instance
of that idea, chosen here for the guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:rotations} and
not for diversity. Section~\ref{sec:orders} bounds what diversity could add
for the components used here, by enumerating the orderings the diverse
selection would be drawn from. Combining components of different families under one
saturated partition is likewise established practice in that
literature. Accordingly, no claim of novelty is made for the constructions
themselves. What this article contributes is an independent implementation
inside a verification framework where $\hstar$ is available exactly, the
resulting confirmation of admissibility and consistency by exhaustive
enumeration instead of by argument alone, and the quantitative attribution in
Section~\ref{sec:anatomy} of the margin obtained by a heterogeneous partition
to the distribution of cost mass among its components.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three cost partitioning schemes over the same set of goal-pattern databases
were implemented and verified against exact goal distances. A uniform partition
is admissible and is the least accurate heuristic measured. A single saturated
ordering is admissible, attains the mean informedness of the maximum over its
own components, and expands substantially more nodes, because it does not
dominate that maximum; a witness state was exhibited and the mechanism
identified. The maximum over the cyclic rotations of the ordering was proved to
dominate the maximum over the components at every state and improves
informedness by $23.9\%$ and expansions by $28.7\%$. A heterogeneous chain
combining the databases with a landmark bound dominates both admissible
controls by construction and improves on the stronger of them by $1.9\%$ of
informedness.

The size of that final margin was traced to the distribution of cost mass: the
databases retain roughly three quarters of the total action cost while yielding
the better bound at one state in five. Saturation is optimal for an isolated
component and is not optimal for a chain.

Enumerating every ordering at the sizes where $k!$ is tractable establishes
where the accuracy of the construction is not. The best single ordering,
selected with full knowledge of the state space, is approximately $20\%$ below
the rotation family, so no procedure that searches for one ordering can replace
it; and the rotation family already recovers $98.6\%$ to $99.2\%$ of the
maximum over all orderings, so no larger family can add more than about one per
cent. What the construction is worth is due to taking a maximum over a family
covering every component. Two directions therefore remain: establishing an
optimal cost partitioning reference point, which would say how much of the
attainable bound saturation recovers, and improving the components themselves,
for which the pattern selection procedure used here is the obvious candidate.

\section*{Reproducibility}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Reproducibility}

The implementation, the benchmark instances and the measurement scripts are
contained in the \code{cost-partitioning} branch of the project repository.
Both command-line tools emit a single self-describing JSON record carrying the
build provenance, the resource limits and every quantity reported above. The
search engine is deterministic: tie-breaking in the open list is by insertion
order, and patterns are a deterministic function of the task and the budget, so
repeated runs of a configuration return identical metrics. The four instances
are generated from a fixed seed and are committed. The enumeration of
Section~\ref{sec:orders} is performed by a separate tool in the same
repository, which agrees with the verifier to four decimal places on the two
quantities they share, the maximum over components and the rotation family, on
all eight instances of five and six blocks.
